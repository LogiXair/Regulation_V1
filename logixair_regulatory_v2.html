Section 01  ·  Classification

Not a Drone.
An Aircraft.

The L560's classification as an aircraft — not a UAV or drone — is the single most important regulatory decision. It defines every certification pathway, every authority to engage, and every standard to design for.

✕
What we are NOT

Drone / UAV

Operating under drone regulations would mean restricted altitude, limited payload, VLOS requirements, and no commercial cargo approval. This path simply doesn't exist for the L560.

  • ✕ Drone Rules 2021 (India) — not applicable
  • ✕ EASA Open Category — max 120 m AGL
  • ✕ EASA Specific Category / SORA — prototype testing only
  • ✕ UIN / UAOP — for small drones only
  • ✕ LUC — Light UAS Operator Certificate
✓
What we ARE

Uncrewed Cargo eVTOL Aircraft

Commercially operated, BVLOS, automated aircraft in shared airspace. Regulated like manned general aviation — but without a pilot onboard.

  • ✓ EASA Special Condition VTOL (SC-VTOL)
  • ✓ Uncrewed Aircraft Special Conditions
  • ✓ Air Operator Certificate (AOC) framework
  • ✓ DGCA Type Certificate — SC-VTOL aligned
  • ✓ EU Regulation 2019/947 + 2021/664
Section 02  ·  Certification Architecture

Three Layers.
All Must Be Certified.

Regulators evaluate the aircraft, the autonomy system, and the operation as three independent workstreams. Getting any one wrong delays the entire program.

1
Aircraft Certification

SC-VTOL + Uncrewed Special Conditions

Certifies the physical aircraft — structure, propulsion, energy management, tilt-wing mechanism, and flight characteristics. The L560's 6+2 distributed tilt-wing configuration falls squarely under EASA SC-VTOL. Avionics from Honeywell and Collins, and aviation-grade certified composites, directly accelerate this layer.

Structural Loads Tilt-Wing Mechanism 6+2 Rotor Failure Analysis Hybrid Power System Flight Control Redundancy Hardest: Transition Safety
2
Autonomy System Certification

The System That Replaces the Pilot

Because no pilot sits onboard, regulators must certify the system replacing the pilot's judgment. This covers decision logic, navigation resilience, command & control link reliability, lost-link procedures, and detect-and-avoid. Usually the longest certification workstream — start immediately.

Flight Management System DAA — Honeywell Radar + ADS-B In GNSS + INS + Radar Navigation C2: Ground + SATCOM Backup Lost-Link Procedure Target: 10⁻⁷ Failure Prob.
3
Operational Certification

Route + Operation + Fleet

Even after the aircraft is certified, each operation requires independent approval. LogiXair's predefined corridor model — certifying aircraft + route + ConOps together as an Operational Design Domain — dramatically reduces this burden. Takeoff from any cleared yard or house forecourt; land at a destination warehouse. No runway or vertiport infrastructure required. Each 600 km corridor becomes its own certified ODD.

Corridor Route Certification Airspace Integration (NOC/NOTAM) Contingency Landing Areas Air Operator Certificate Remote Pilot Supervision Human-on-the-Loop Model

Section 04  ·  Command & Control

Always Connected.
Never Dependent.

A dual-link C2 architecture with deterministic lost-link procedures ensures the aircraft remains safe even when connectivity fails. Regulators will scrutinise every link failure scenario.

📡
Primary Link — Ground Network
High-bandwidth ground-based C2 distributed along corridor. Full telemetry, video, and command capability. Continuous automatic handoff between ground monitoring stations.
Full Bandwidth · Full Telemetry
🛰
Backup Link — SATCOM
Activated automatically on primary link degradation. Lower bandwidth — carries only critical flight commands. Prevents premature lost-link activation.
Critical Commands Only
⚡
Lost-Link Procedure
Pre-certified, deterministic, non-negotiable. Activated only after both links confirm lost. Aircraft proceeds to nearest certified contingency area and lands autonomously. No deviation permitted under any circumstances.
👁
Human-on-the-Loop Supervision
One remote supervisory pilot monitors multiple aircraft. Can intervene, override, or initiate emergency landing at any time. Currently the most regulator-accepted autonomy model worldwide.

Section 06  ·  Hardest Certification Areas

Five Challenges.
All Addressable.

These are the areas where most autonomous cargo aircraft programs lose years. Addressing them early — with the right architecture and supplier partnerships — separates a 5-year certification from a 10-year one.

01
🔄
Highest Risk

Tilt-Wing Transition Safety

The transition from VTOL hover to wing-borne cruise — and back — is the most complex, highest-risk flight phase. Regulators require complete failure mode analysis for every transition scenario including actuator failure and asymmetric tilt.

Mitigation
Early test program focused exclusively on transition envelope mapping
EU-granted patent on H 6+2 tilt-wing configuration provides baseline IP and engineering confidence
Address in PSSA before certification basis is agreed with DGCA
02
⚙️
High Risk

Distributed Propulsion Failure

6+2 distributed rotors require complete failure mode analysis. Regulators will require demonstration of safe flight and controlled landing after worst-case single and dual rotor failure — especially during hover.

Mitigation
Modular Adaptive Energy Storage System (patent pending) enables multi-state rotor redundancy
Failure tolerance demonstration is an early test milestone — not deferred
03
📡
High Risk · Long Duration

Detect-and-Avoid Validation

DAA is the most time-consuming certification workstream. Regulators require statistical validation — thousands of encounter scenarios, hardware-in-the-loop testing, and cooperative flight tests with manned aircraft before approval.

Mitigation
Honeywell radar provides proven aviation-grade safety case data — inheritable
Begin DAA simulation campaign in Year 1 of L560 program — not Year 3
3-layer DAA: ADS-B In + Radar + Route strategic deconfliction
04
📶
Medium Risk

Lost-Link Procedure Certification

The lost-link procedure must be pre-defined, deterministic, and certified. Regulators will not accept ambiguous or situation-dependent behaviour. Must work even with partial navigation degradation.

Mitigation
Define and freeze lost-link logic during architecture phase — not post-build
Multiple certified contingency landing areas across every 600 km corridor
05
🛡️
Medium Risk · Foundational

System Safety Assessment (PSSA → SSA)

PSSA must be completed before the certification basis is agreed; full SSA before approval. At 1,350 kg MTOW operating BVLOS in shared airspace, catastrophic failure probability targets are stringent (10⁻⁷ to 10⁻⁹ per flight hour). Every system — propulsion, flight control, navigation, C2, energy, autonomy — must be covered.

LogiXair Strategic Advantages
Honeywell and Collins avionics provide existing certified safety cases — avoids building from scratch
Aviation-grade certified composite structures — structural safety case substantially inherited
Fuel-agnostic hybrid powertrain (patent pending) — energy system redundancy architecturally embedded

Section 08  ·  Immediate Action Items

Six Actions.
Start Now.

The regulatory momentum established in the next 6 months determines whether LogiXair certifies in 2028 or 2031. These actions run in parallel — none can wait for another to complete.

01
Initiate Pre-Application Consultation with DGCA
Request early engagement to introduce the L560 program. Present aircraft classification (not drone), proposed certification basis (SC-VTOL aligned), and ConOps overview. Goal: DGCA agrees on regulatory pathway and issues guidance on Special Conditions process.
02
Commission Preliminary System Safety Assessment (PSSA)
Engage aerospace safety consultant to initiate PSSA aligned with ARP4761. Identify all catastrophic failure scenarios before architecture is frozen. PSSA output feeds directly into Certification Basis Proposal — do not wait for L560 design to be complete.
03
Prepare Certification Basis Proposal
Draft document defining: SC-VTOL requirements applicable to L560, proposed Special Conditions for uncrewed operation, and means of compliance for each requirement. This is submitted to DGCA to formally open the Type Certificate program.
04
Begin DAA Validation Planning
Define encounter scenarios for simulation campaign. Confirm Honeywell radar specification and existing aviation safety case availability. Plan flight test program for DAA demonstration. This workstream takes 2–3 years minimum — it starts now.
05
Formalise the ConOps Document
Expand ConOps framework into a full regulatory submission document per ICAO Doc 10019 / EASA AMC guidance. Include route survey methodology, contingency area criteria, weather minima, and pilot qualification framework. Becomes the reference for all regulatory submissions.
06
Initiate Informal EASA Engagement
Make initial contact with EASA to introduce LogiXair program. Discuss potential DGCA-EASA bilateral arrangement for mutual recognition of certification data. Identify EASA project certification manager. Indian operational data will be the key accelerator for European approval.
